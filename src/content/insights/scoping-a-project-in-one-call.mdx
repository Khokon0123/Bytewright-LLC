Most projects arrive as a feeling: *our site is out of date*, *we need an app*, *can AI help with this?* The first call is where we turn that feeling into something you can plan and price. We ask five questions, in roughly this order.

## 1. What changes if this works?

Not features. Outcomes. More enquiries, fewer support emails, a team that stops copying data between spreadsheets. If we can name the change, we can design for it.

## 2. Who uses it, and where?

A monastery’s members reading on their phones after a service need something different from a contractor checking a quote on a laptop. The answer shapes the design more than any style preference.

## 3. What exists today?

Content, tools, logins, data, a domain someone registered in 2014. Knowing what we inherit keeps estimates honest.

## 4. What’s fixed, and what’s flexible?

A launch date, a budget range, a board meeting. When we know the hard edges, we can be creative inside them.

## 5. What happens after launch?

Who updates it, who answers when something breaks, and what the next version might be. Software lives on after the handoff, so we plan for it from the start.
