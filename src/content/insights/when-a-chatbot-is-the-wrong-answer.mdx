A lot of AI requests start with *we need a chatbot*. Sometimes that’s right. Often the real problem is something smaller, cheaper, and more reliable.

## It might be search

If people ask a chatbot the same twenty questions, they may just need a page that answers them well, with search that understands how they phrase things.

## It might be a form

If the conversation always ends with collecting a name, a date, and a request, a well-designed form is faster for the user and easier to trust.

## It might be automation

If someone spends an hour a day moving information from email to a spreadsheet to an invoice, the win is a workflow that does that for them. No chat window required.

## When a chatbot *is* right

- The questions are varied and the answers live in documents you already have.
- People need help in the moment, outside office hours.
- You can measure whether it’s actually helping, and you’re ready to improve it.

> The best AI feature is the one people don’t have to think about.

We start every AI project with a short audit: what people are trying to do, where the time goes, and what would genuinely help. Sometimes the answer is AI. Sometimes it’s a better button.
